# SehatSaathi India Market Survey

Date: 2026-05-22  
Scope: Desk research only. This is a strategy memo based on current public sources, not primary field interviews or paid survey work.

## Executive verdict

SehatSaathi appears to target a real problem, but not in the way it may first seem.

The real problem is not "people need AI to replace doctors." The real problem is:

- diagnostic reports often reach patients as static documents
- follow-up after the report is fragmented
- patients do not know what to keep ready for the next visit
- labs usually stop at delivery, not continuity

That means the strongest opportunity is not a broad consumer health app. It is a narrower continuity layer between diagnostics and next-step preparation.

The opportunity looks real. The current market entry is the weak part.

## Bottom-line answer

### Can this solve a real problem?

Yes, probably.

But the problem it solves best is:

- post-report continuity
- patient preparation before follow-up
- structured organization of repeated reports
- a lab/clinic retention and engagement layer

Not:

- doctor replacement
- diagnosis
- treatment planning
- broad D2C health behavior app

### Is there a wedge?

Yes, but it is narrow.

The most plausible wedge is:

Lab-branded post-report follow-up support for chronic and repeat-testing patients.

Secondary wedge:

Follow-up preparation for chronic care clinics where patients bring repeated lab reports over time.

### Should you shut it down now?

No. Not yet.

But you should not keep treating the product as a broad consumer app either.

You need a narrow proof point quickly:

- one partner type
- one workflow
- one measurable result

If that does not get pull, then you should seriously consider pausing or killing it.

## Why the problem is real

### 1. India has a very large chronic-disease and repeat-testing base

ICMR-INdiaB estimates around 101 million people with diabetes and another 136 million with prediabetes in India. That is a massive repeat-testing and repeat-follow-up population. Obesity, hypertension, thyroid disorders, kidney disease, and inflammatory conditions increase the same pattern: more reports, more fragmentation, and more need for continuity.

Source:
- ICMR-INdiaB summary via PIB: https://pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1941471

### 2. Diagnostics is large, but the patient experience is still document-heavy

The Indian diagnostics market is large and still highly operational. Major chains talk about report reach, collection logistics, network density, and customer convenience, but the post-report layer is still mostly report delivery, doctor referral, or health packages.

Representative company references:
- Dr Lal PathLabs annual/investor materials: https://www.lalpathlabs.com/investors
- Redcliffe Labs: https://redcliffelabs.com/
- Orange Health: https://www.orangehealth.in/

This matters because SehatSaathi is not competing with "whether a report can be delivered." It is competing with "what happens after delivery."

### 3. India is actively building digital health infrastructure

ABDM has built national rails for digital health records, ABHA-linked identity, registries, and interoperable health data movement. By early 2025, official reporting indicated hundreds of millions of ABHA accounts and large-scale transaction growth. That means the health system is becoming more digitally legible, but continuity still needs product layers on top.

Sources:
- ABDM official: https://www.abdm.gov.in/
- PIB update on ABDM scale: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2101737

### 4. The continuity gap is still under-served

Most patient-facing health products in India cluster around:

- booking tests
- ordering medicines
- teleconsults
- storing records
- health packages

Much fewer products are centered on:

- report-to-follow-up continuity
- lab-driven patient preparation
- structured next-visit readiness
- repeated comparison and retest framing

That is where SehatSaathi has a potential wedge.

## Why the market is still hard

### 1. "AI explains your report" is a crowded and mistrusted category

If SehatSaathi is heard as:

- AI interpretation app
- AI report explainer
- AI action planner

then it becomes commercially weak very quickly because:

- patients may mistrust it
- labs may fear liability
- clinicians may see doctor-replacement risk
- many adjacent products already offer some version of report storage, health records, or doctor Q&A

### 2. Labs do not wake up wanting "action plans"

Labs want:

- more repeat business
- better patient retention
- stronger brand relationship
- lower support burden
- no clinical liability
- no staff complexity

If SehatSaathi sounds like it is moving from:

report delivery -> clinical recommendation

then labs get nervous.

If it sounds like:

report delivery -> clearer patient preparation and continuity

they may listen.

### 3. Consumer acquisition is probably a trap at this stage

This category usually has expensive consumer acquisition because:

- trust is hard
- urgency is episodic
- retention is not naturally daily
- users do not want another app unless the entry moment is perfect

That makes D2C marketing risky unless there is a strong referral loop or partner-triggered usage.

## Current market landscape

### 1. Labs and diagnostics players

Examples:

- Dr Lal PathLabs
- Metropolis
- SRL
- Redcliffe Labs
- Orange Health
- Tata 1mg Labs

What they do well:

- collection and fulfillment
- report delivery
- test discovery
- home collection

What they do less well:

- follow-up continuity after delivery
- repeat context across reports for the patient
- patient prep for next review in a neutral, structured way

### 2. Personal health-record apps

Examples:

- Eka Care
- ABDM-linked health record products
- hospital apps and portals

What they do well:

- storage
- access
- record organization

What they often do less well:

- converting records into a practical continuity workflow

### 3. Doctor-first or telehealth products

Examples:

- Practo
- hospital apps
- clinic CRMs

What they do well:

- appointments
- consultations
- provider workflow

What they often do less well:

- patient-side continuity between report receipt and next review

## What SehatSaathi is actually best at

The strongest job-to-be-done is not:

"tell me what disease I have"

It is:

"I got a lab report. Help me understand what to keep in view, what to ask, and how to stay organized before the next follow-up."

That is a meaningful job.

## Where the wedge is strongest

### Best wedge: independent labs and small-to-mid diagnostic groups

Why this wedge is strongest:

- labs already own the report moment
- patient attention is highest right after the report
- repeat-testing patients are valuable
- labs need differentiation beyond price and logistics
- SehatSaathi can layer on top of existing PDF/report workflows

What the pitch should be:

"We help your patients receive reports with better follow-up clarity and continuity, without changing your clinical role."

Not:

"We give AI medical action plans."

### Second-best wedge: chronic clinics

Especially:

- diabetes clinics
- endocrinology clinics
- dermatology clinics with inflammation-heavy repeat patients
- nephrology / chronic follow-up workflows

Why:

- repeated labs are already core to the visit pattern
- continuity is obvious
- patient value is easier to explain

### Weak wedge: broad consumer app

Why weak:

- high CAC risk
- low trust by default
- unclear habit loop
- not strong enough as a generic consumer destination

## Will people adopt it?

### Patients

Possible if:

- triggered by a report they already received
- branded by a lab or clinic they already trust
- framed as preparation and continuity, not self-treatment

Weak if:

- cold app download
- generic AI-health messaging
- too much reading

### Labs

Possible if:

- low staff burden
- low liability perception
- improves retention or brand differentiation
- easy workflow integration

Weak if:

- sounds clinical
- requires staff explanation every time
- slows report ops

## What the product must stop sounding like

To reduce resistance, avoid leading with:

- action plan
- AI interpretation
- medical guidance
- review first
- what this means for your condition

Use instead:

- report follow-up support
- next-visit preparation
- continuity between reports
- questions to discuss
- organized follow-up context

## Commercial viability

### Could this become a business?

Yes, if it becomes B2B2C or clinic/lab-led.

Much weaker if it stays consumer-first.

### Most believable revenue paths

1. Per-branch lab SaaS
2. Base fee + usage
3. White-labeled continuity layer for labs/clinics
4. Chronic clinic workflow support

### Weak revenue path

Paid consumer app subscriptions at the beginning.

## What counts as proof of concept

Not:

- lots of features
- good design
- one founder dry run

Real proof of concept means:

- one partner type
- one workflow
- repeated use
- a measurable outcome

Example:

For one lab branch:

- 50-100 patients receive report-linked follow-up view
- open rate and engagement are measured
- lab staff burden is measured
- partner wants to continue after pilot

That is real proof.

## The biggest risk

The biggest risk is not that the problem is fake.

The biggest risk is that the market interprets the product as:

- clinical advice
- doctor replacement
- extra complexity

before it sees the continuity value.

That is a positioning problem.

## Founder decision framework

### Continue if

- at least one buyer group clearly understands the narrow use case
- one partner will pilot it
- the workflow can be triggered from the report moment
- the product can be sold without doctor-replacement fear

### Pause or kill if

- labs do not care even after safer framing
- clinics do not care even after narrowing
- patients only like it in theory but do not use it
- there is no measurable pull after a real narrow pilot attempt

## My blunt recommendation

For the next 2-4 weeks:

1. Reposition the product as a continuity layer, not an AI action engine.
2. Pick one buyer only:
   - independent labs, or
   - diabetes/derm chronic clinics
3. Run 8-12 structured conversations.
4. Offer one narrow pilot with one workflow.
5. Measure:
   - partner willingness
   - patient open/use
   - repeat usage
   - perceived liability
6. Decide quickly after that.

Do not keep building broad product surfaces while the wedge is still unclear.

## Final verdict

SehatSaathi does appear to target a real problem in India.

But the real opportunity is narrower than a general health app:

- post-report continuity
- next-visit preparation
- repeated-lab follow-up organization

That can be innovative and commercially meaningful.

What is not yet proven is whether labs or clinics care enough in a way that is easy to sell.

So the product is not dead.

But it is not validated either.

The next right move is not more product building. It is wedge validation.
